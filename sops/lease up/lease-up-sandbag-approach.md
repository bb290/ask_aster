Situation: the current Resident declines occupied showings, or the unit is not in showing condition. Prospect interest still has to be managed so the listing does not go cold.

The Sandbag Approach strategically delays showings while keeping prospects engaged.

## Key Points

- **The home is cluttered or dirty** — a bad showing costs more than a delayed one. A prospect who walks a messy unit rarely comes back after turn over.
- **The Resident is hostile or uncooperative** — never force access. Resident cooperation is a condition of occupied showings, not a negotiation.
- **The unit is inaccessible or disrupted by move prep** — boxes, disassembled furniture, and packed hallways do not show well.

## Procedure

1. Schedule showings to begin 1 to 2 days after Move Out. This leaves time to inspect and adjust if condition is worse than expected.
2. Batch showings: stack multiple prospects into the same time block.
3. Communicate transparently with prospects. Never imply the unit is unavailable, already leased, or off market.
4. Set expectations about condition before the showing so nobody arrives expecting a finished unit.

## Prospect Inquiry

Reply to the original inquiry thread.

> Thanks so much for your interest! The current Residents have requested that I wait until after they move out to begin showings, and our company policy is to respect Resident privacy as much as possible.
>
> They're scheduled to vacate on [Move Out Date], so we've set the first showing for [Showing Date]. Would 2pm work for you, or is 6pm better?
>
> Please note the home may not be fully move-in ready at the time of your showing. Minor repairs and professional cleaning may still be in progress. You're absolutely welcome to view the space to get a feel for the layout and features.

### Example Prospect Questions

> "Can I see it this week?"

> "Why can't I tour it if it's listed?"

> "Is it still available? I haven't been able to get in to see it."
